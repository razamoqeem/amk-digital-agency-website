How to write title tags that get clicked

The title tag is often the first thing a searcher reads. Here is how Google uses it and how to write one that is clear, unique and worth clicking.

By KivoReach · Published 18 November 2026 · 3 min read · 512 words

On this page
  1. Where Google gets the title it shows
  2. What makes a good title
  3. How long should a title be?
  4. A simple method
  5. Check your titles

The title tag is the <title> element in a page's code. Browsers show it on the tab, and search engines often use it as the clickable headline in their results. A good title tells people what the page is about before they click.

Where Google gets the title it shows

Google calls the headline in its results a title link. It is generated automatically. The page's <title> element is one of its sources, but Google can also use the main visible title on the page, other headings, the og:title tag, and the text of links pointing to the page.

Note

Because the title link is generated, you cannot force a particular title. You can make your <title> the clearest option, which makes it the most likely one to be used.

What makes a good title

Good and weak title patterns
DoAvoidWhy
Describe the page in plain wordsVague titles such as "Home" or "Untitled"Searchers need to know what they will find
Write a different title for every pageThe same title on many pagesRepeated titles make pages hard to tell apart
Keep it conciseLong lists of keywordsKeyword stuffing reads badly and can be rewritten by Google
Add your brand once, brieflyLong repeated boilerplate on every titleBoilerplate pushes the useful words out of view

How long should a title be?

Google has no fixed limit on the length of a <title> element. In results, a title link is shortened as needed, usually to fit the width of the device. In practice, a title that is too long loses its last words, so put the most important words at the start.

A simple method

  1. Name the topic

    Start with the main subject of the page, in the words your visitors use.

  2. Add what makes the page useful

    A benefit, a format or a detail: "guide", "price list", "for beginners", a place name.

  3. Add your brand if there is room

    Usually at the end, separated by a short divider such as | or -.

  4. Check it

    Read it as a searcher would. Is it clear, specific and different from your other pages?

Check your titles

  • Every page has exactly one <title> element
  • No two important pages share the same title
  • The main topic appears near the start
  • The title matches what the page actually offers
  • No lists of repeated keywords

Does changing a title guarantee a new headline in Google?

No. Google generates the title link and may choose other text from the page. A clear, accurate title makes it more likely that your own title is used.

Should the title and the H1 be the same?

They do not have to be identical, but they should describe the same topic. Google may use the main heading as a source, so a heading that matches the page's purpose helps.

References

  1. Influencing your title links in search results — Google Search Central
  2. SEO Starter Guide — Google Search Central

Key takeaways

  • Every page needs its own clear, descriptive <title>.
  • Google can show a different title link if yours is unclear, stuffed or repeated.
  • There is no fixed character limit; long titles are shortened to fit the screen, so put the key words first.
