Local SEO for small business: a practical guide

If you serve customers in an area, local SEO helps you appear when people nearby search. Here is how Google ranks local results and what a small business can do.

By KivoReach · Published 19 November 2026 · 4 min read · 758 words

On this page
  1. How Google ranks local results
  2. Step 1: set up your Google Business Profile
  3. Step 2: earn and answer reviews
  4. Step 3: make your website local-friendly
  5. Step 4: get mentioned locally
  6. Local SEO checklist

Local SEO helps a business appear when people search for something nearby, such as a plumber, a dentist or a coffee shop, or a search that ends in "near me". For a small business that serves a town or region, it is often the most valuable kind of SEO, because the people searching are close and ready to act.

How Google ranks local results

Google's Business Profile help pages say local results are mainly based on three things:

Local ranking factors, as Google describes them
FactorWhat it meansWhat you can do
RelevanceHow well your Business Profile matches what someone searched forGive complete, detailed business information and the right category
DistanceHow far your business is from the person searching (or the location Google knows for them)Nothing to change; keep your address or service area accurate
Prominence (also called popularity)How well known your business is, including links to your business and your reviewsEarn reviews, reply to them, and get mentioned by local sites

Note

Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Be wary of anyone who promises one.

Step 1: set up your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that can appear in Google Maps and in the local results of Google Search. If you do not have one yet, you can add or claim it for free.

  1. Add or claim your profile

    Search for your business on Google Maps. If it exists, claim it; if not, add it, then complete verification.

  2. Fill in every detail

    Business name (as used in real life), address or service area, phone, website, opening hours including special hours, and the category that best describes you.

  3. Add photos

    Show your premises, products, team or work. Real photos help customers know what to expect.

  4. Keep it current

    Update hours for holidays and changes, so customers never arrive to a closed door.

Warning

Follow Google's guidelines for representing your business: use your real business name without extra keywords, and a real address or service area. Breaking them can get a profile suspended.

Step 2: earn and answer reviews

Reviews feed into prominence, and they help customers decide. Ask satisfied customers for a review, make it easy with a direct link, and reply to reviews, both good and bad, politely and helpfully. Never buy or fake reviews.

Step 3: make your website local-friendly

  • Same details everywhere: your name, address and phone on the website should match your Business Profile.
  • One page per service: describe each service clearly, and the area you serve, in your customers' words.
  • Clear titles: for example "Emergency Plumber in [Your Town] | [Business Name]".
  • Contact details people can find: on every page, usually in the footer, plus a contact page.
  • Structured data: LocalBusiness markup tells Google your hours, address and other details in a format it reads directly.
  • Phone-ready pages: most local searches happen on phones, so pages must load and read well on a small screen.

Tip

Check your page's titles with our Meta Tag Analyser, and see whether your structured data is present and readable with the Schema and Open Graph Checker.

Step 4: get mentioned locally

Links and mentions from local sources help Google see you as part of the community: local news, business associations, event pages, suppliers and partners. Earn them with real activity, such as sponsoring a local event or sharing useful local advice, not by buying them.

Local SEO checklist

  • Google Business Profile added or claimed and verified
  • Category, hours, address or service area, phone and website complete and correct
  • Real photos added
  • A simple way for customers to leave reviews, and replies to reviews
  • Website shows the same name, address and phone as the profile
  • One clear page for each main service
  • LocalBusiness structured data on the website
  • Pages work well on phones
Do I need a website for local SEO?

A Business Profile can appear without one, but a website gives Google and customers much more to go on: your services, your area and your proof of quality. Linking the two also helps customers take the next step.

I work from home and visit customers. Do I show my address?

No. Google's guidelines say a service-area business should hide its address from customers and show the areas it serves instead.

How long does local SEO take?

Profile changes can show quickly, while reviews and reputation build over months. Google does not publish a timeline, and anyone who guarantees one is guessing.

Key takeaways

  • Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence (how well known a business is).
  • A complete, accurate Google Business Profile is the starting point.
  • There is no way to pay for a better local ranking on Google; reviews, accurate details and a useful website are what you can work on.
