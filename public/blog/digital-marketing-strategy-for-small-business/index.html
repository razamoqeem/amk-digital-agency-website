Digital marketing strategy for small business: a simple plan

A digital marketing strategy does not need to be long. Here is a one-page plan a small business can write in an afternoon and actually follow.

By KivoReach · Published 19 November 2026 · 4 min read · 668 words

On this page
  1. The one-page plan
  2. Step by step
  3. Common channels for small businesses
  4. How to measure without paying for tools
  5. Strategy checklist

A digital marketing strategy is a plan for how your business will reach and win customers online: who you want to reach, where, with what message, and how you will know it works. For a small business or a beginner, the best strategy is short, specific and realistic about time and budget.

The one-page plan

Five questions that make up a basic strategy
QuestionExample answer for a local bakery
What do we want?More weekday pre-orders for cakes
Who is the customer?Parents and offices within 10 km, planning birthdays and events
Where do they look?Google Search and Maps, Instagram, WhatsApp
What do we tell them?Order online by 5 pm for next-day pickup; photos of real cakes
How do we measure it?Pre-orders per week, website visits from Google, calls from the Business Profile

Step by step

  1. Set one or two goals

    Tie them to the business: more enquiries, bookings or sales of a specific service. "Be on social media" is an activity, not a goal.

  2. Describe your customer

    Who they are, what problem they have, the words they search with, and what makes them choose you.

  3. Fix your home base

    Your website and Google Business Profile are where every channel sends people. Make sure they load fast, work on phones and make the next step obvious.

  4. Pick two or three channels

    Choose the ones your customers already use. It is better to do two well than six badly.

  5. Plan simple content

    Answer your customers' common questions, show your work, and share offers. A small, steady schedule beats bursts.

  6. Measure and adjust monthly

    Check what brought visits and enquiries, keep what works, and drop what does not.

Common channels for small businesses

What each channel is good for
ChannelGood forCosts mainly
SEO (search)People actively looking for what you offerTime; results build over months
Google Business ProfileLocal customers searching nearbyTime; the profile is free
Social mediaShowing your work, building familiarityTime, and money if you boost posts
EmailRepeat customers and offersTime and an email tool; only email people who agreed to it
Paid search and social adsQuick visibility while SEO buildsMoney per click or view

New to SEO? Start with our on-page vs off-page SEO guide, and if you serve a local area, read local SEO for small business.

How to measure without paying for tools

Google Search Console is free and shows which searches bring people to your site, which pages appear in results and any problems Google finds. Your Business Profile shows calls, direction requests and website clicks. Write down the numbers once a month in a simple sheet next to your goals.

Note

Be careful with promises of fixed results. No one can guarantee rankings or sales, and Google states that it does not accept payment to rank a site higher in its search results.

Strategy checklist

  • One or two goals written down, tied to sales or enquiries
  • A short description of your main customer and the words they use
  • Website and Business Profile ready to receive visitors
  • Two or three channels chosen, with a realistic weekly time budget
  • A simple content plan for the next month
  • Search Console set up and a monthly review in the calendar
How much should a small business spend on digital marketing?

There is no standard figure that fits every business. Start with the time you can give every week, and add a small, fixed ad budget only for a clear goal you can measure.

Should I do SEO or ads first?

Many small businesses do both: ads bring visits now, while SEO builds visits that do not cost per click. If the budget is zero, start with your Business Profile and the on-page basics of your website.

How often should I change my strategy?

Review it monthly and change the plan when the numbers show something is not working. Change the goals only when the business changes.

Key takeaways

  • Start from one or two business goals, not from a list of channels.
  • Pick the few channels your customers already use and do them well.
  • Measure from the start with free tools such as Google Search Console, and review monthly.
