301 vs 302 redirects: which one to use

Moving a page? The type of redirect tells search engines whether the move is permanent. Here is how to choose, and how to keep redirects short.

By AMK Digital Agency · Published 18 November 2026 · 2 min read · 384 words

On this page
  1. Permanent or temporary
  2. Keep redirect chains short
  3. Moving a page: step by step

A redirect sends visitors and search engines from one address to another. It is how you keep old links working when a page moves, a site changes domain or you switch from http to https.

Permanent or temporary

Server-side redirects come with a status code. The code tells search engines whether the move is permanent or temporary, and that changes which address Google shows in its results.

Redirect status codes and what they signal
CodeTypeWhat it tells GoogleUse it when
301PermanentThe new address should be shown in resultsA page or site has moved for good
308PermanentSame as 301A permanent move where the request method must not change
302TemporaryKeep showing the original addressA page is briefly elsewhere and will come back
303TemporaryKeep showing the original addressSending a visitor to another page after a form
307TemporaryKeep showing the original addressA temporary move where the method must not change

Warning

A common mistake is using a temporary 302 for a permanent move. The original address can then stay in search results instead of the new one.

Keep redirect chains short

A chain is when one redirect leads to another, and another. Google's crawlers follow up to 10 redirect hops by default, but each extra hop slows visitors down and adds a chance of failure. Point every old address straight to its final destination.

Moving a page: step by step

  1. List the old and new addresses

    One line per page that moves.

  2. Add permanent redirects

    From each old address directly to its new address.

  3. Update your own links

    Change internal links and your sitemap to the new addresses, so visitors do not need the redirect.

  4. Test every old address

    Check that each one reaches the right page in one hop with a 200 status at the end.

Our free Redirect and Status Checker follows any address hop by hop and shows every status code until the final page.

How long should I keep a redirect?

Keep permanent redirects in place for as long as people and other sites might still use the old address. Removing them too early breaks old links.

References

  1. Redirects and Google Search — Google Search Central
  2. How HTTP status codes, and network and DNS errors affect Google Search — Google Search Central

Key takeaways

  • Use a permanent redirect (301 or 308) when a page has moved for good.
  • Use a temporary redirect (302, 303 or 307) only when the original address will come back.
  • Keep chains short: Google's crawlers follow up to 10 redirect hops by default.
