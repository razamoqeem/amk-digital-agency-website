What are backlinks? How to get them safely

Backlinks are links from other websites to yours. They help search engines find and judge your pages, but buying them breaks Google's rules. Here is how to earn them.

By KivoReach · Published 19 November 2026 · 4 min read · 664 words

On this page
  1. Why backlinks matter
  2. Good links and risky links
  3. Safe ways to get backlinks
  4. What about "domain authority" scores?
  5. Backlink checklist

A backlink is a link on another website that points to yours. If a local news site links to your shop's page, that is a backlink for you. Backlinks are the best-known part of off-page SEO, and also the part where most people get bad advice.

Why backlinks matter

Search engines discover new pages by following links, so a link from a known site helps your page get found. Google's guide to how Search works describes links as one of the ways it finds pages. Links are also a sign of attention: people link to things they find useful. That is why a few links from relevant, trusted sites are worth more than many from unrelated or low-quality ones.

Good links and risky links

How Google's policies separate earned links from link spam
Usually fineLink spam under Google's policies
A site links to your page because it is usefulPaying money, goods or services for links that pass ranking credit
A local paper writes about your businessExcessive link exchanges ("link to me and I'll link to you")
A partner lists you, with the link marked as sponsored if paidAutomated programs or services that create links to your site
You are listed in a genuine, relevant directoryLow-quality directory or bookmark site links
Your guest article helps readers, with links marked appropriatelyKeyword-stuffed links in articles, guest posts or press releases spread across many sites

Warning

Google's spam policies define link spam as creating links mainly to manipulate rankings. If a link is paid for or sponsored, it should carry rel="sponsored" (or nofollow) so it passes no ranking credit.

Safe ways to get backlinks

  1. Publish something worth linking to

    A clear guide, a free tool, local data you collected, or a page that answers a common question better than anyone else.

  2. Tell the people who care

    Share it with communities, partners and customers who would find it useful, without asking for a link in return.

  3. Get listed where customers look

    Your Google Business Profile, your industry's genuine directories and local business associations.

  4. Work with real partners

    Suppliers, clients and event organisers often list the businesses they work with.

  5. Fix broken links to you

    If someone links to a page of yours that moved, ask them to update it, or redirect the old address to the new one.

Use our free Broken Link Checker to make sure the links on your own pages work, and Website Comparison to compare your site with a competitor's.

What about "domain authority" scores?

Scores such as Domain Authority or Domain Rating are made by private SEO companies to estimate a site's link strength. They are not Google measures. They can be handy for comparing sites, but improving a third-party score is not the goal; earning real links from relevant sites is.

Backlink checklist

  • You have at least one page that is genuinely useful to link to
  • Your Business Profile and real directory listings are complete
  • Paid or sponsored links are marked rel="sponsored" or nofollow
  • No link packages, link swaps or automated link building
  • Old addresses that others link to redirect to the right page
How many backlinks do I need?

There is no number. Google does not publish one, and a few relevant links from trusted sites can matter more than many weak ones. Focus on earning links to your most useful pages.

Can link schemes hurt my site?

Yes. Google's spam policies say it detects violations with automated systems and, when needed, human review, and that sites breaking the policies may rank lower or not appear at all. Do not buy links or join link networks.

Are nofollow links useless?

No. They can still bring visitors and attention. Google's guidance is to mark paid or sponsored links with rel="sponsored" (nofollow is still accepted) so that they pass no ranking credit.

Key takeaways

  • A backlink is a link from another website to yours.
  • Links help search engines discover pages and can signal that content is worth reading.
  • Buying links, excessive link swaps and automated link building are link spam under Google's policies; earn links with useful pages instead.
